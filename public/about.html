About ALI Charity

A donation information site that shows its evidence — and its limits.

ALI Charity publishes official crypto donation addresses, donor-safety instructions, humanitarian project routes and a workflow for retaining public transaction records. A blockchain record can verify that a transfer happened; it does not by itself prove off-chain delivery, legal status, an independent audit or beneficiary impact.

Last reviewed: November 8, 2026 · Maintained by: ALI Charity Operations · Method: entity facts are checked against the Donate page and the canonical site configuration. AI-assisted maintenance is disclosed; factual claims require source review.

Our purpose

ALI Charity helps a donor verify the official domain, asset, network, recipient address and transaction-hash workflow before sending an irreversible crypto transfer. Donors send from their own wallet or exchange and keep the transaction hash. Public transaction records are the first evidence layer; project delivery, spending and beneficiary claims require separate off-chain documentation.

Receipt layer

Public records

A public explorer can show the sender, recipient, asset, amount, timestamp and confirmation status that network records.

Donor control

Your wallet, your signature

Transfers happen entirely in the donor's own wallet or exchange. This website only publishes addresses and verification guidance.

Evidence limit

What a transfer cannot prove

A recorded transfer does not prove who legally controls an address, how funds were spent off-chain or whether aid reached a beneficiary.

ALI Token

No return promises

ALI is donor-recognition context only — not a reward, return, redemption right, exchange listing or governance promise. Token details.

Operating principles

  1. Evidence before claims. No donor counts, totals or impact figures are shown unless a dated public record supports them. Unknown does not mean zero.
  2. Donor wallet control. No wallet connection, no signing requests, no seed-phrase prompts — ever.
  3. One address source. Addresses are published only on the live Donate page from one canonical configuration file.
  4. Honest limitations. Every page that discusses proof also states what that proof does not establish.

What is not currently claimed

This page does not claim that ALI Charity is the first blockchain charity, that a particular number of donors have participated, or that every project expenditure and beneficiary outcome has been independently verified. No independent audit, third-party endorsement or verified off-chain impact report is currently published. If that evidence becomes available, it will be published with dates, responsible parties, source documents and a review status.

Reference basis: the U.S. Federal Trade Commission explains that crypto payments are usually irreversible and commonly recorded on public ledgers (FTC guidance). Google's people-first content guidance asks publishers to make authorship, methods and sourcing clear. Sources accessed August 17, 2026.
