Crypto Donation Safety Checklist (2026)

Verify addresses, avoid impersonation scams, and keep proof before sending an irreversible crypto donation.

Home / Blog / Donor Safety

Crypto donations can be fast and borderless, but they are also irreversible. A safe donation workflow is simple: verify the official source, verify the address, verify the chain, and keep a clean record.

Quick Answer

To donate crypto safely, use the charity's official website, confirm the exact wallet address and network, check token contracts on a block explorer, avoid DMs and lookalike accounts, send a small test transfer when unsure, and keep the transaction hash.

1. Verify you are on the real website

  • Type the domain directly instead of following shortened links.
  • Check the Donate page and at least one additional official page for the same address.
  • Be skeptical of urgent private messages. The FTC warns that scammers often impersonate trusted organizations and push unusual payment methods, including cryptocurrency.

2. Verify the donation address and chain

  • Confirm the chain or network before sending. BNB Chain, Ethereum, Bitcoin, and Solana addresses are not interchangeable.
  • Copy the address from the official site and verify the first and last characters twice.
  • For tokens, confirm the token contract on a recognized block explorer. ALI Token's BNB Smart Chain contract is visible on BscScan.

3. Use a small test transfer when unsure

  • Send a small amount first, then confirm receipt on-chain before sending the full donation.
  • Use stablecoins such as USDT when you want lower price volatility for aid budgeting, but still verify the chain and recipient address.

4. Keep proof for transparency and records

  • Record the transaction hash, recipient address, amount, asset, chain, and timestamp.
  • Save a screenshot or PDF of the donation page used at the time of giving.
  • For personal tax records, keep the fair-market-value and wallet history documentation required in your jurisdiction.

5. Watch for common scam patterns

  • "We changed our donation address, send here instead" without a verifiable official update.
  • Copycat domains, fake support accounts, and social posts using stolen logos.
  • Promises of returns, refunds, doubled donations, or guaranteed token price outcomes.
  • Recovery scams that claim your lost crypto has been traced and ask for fees to release it.
