Crypto Donation Safety Checklist (2026)

Verify addresses, avoid impersonation scams, and keep proof before sending an irreversible crypto donation.

Home / Blog / Donor Safety

Crypto donations can be fast and borderless, but they are also irreversible. A safe donation workflow is simple: verify the official source, verify the address, verify the chain, and keep a clean record.

Quick Answer

Start on the live official Donate page. Check the asset and network, then compare the complete recipient address in your wallet with that page, including the middle characters. Do not copy from transaction history. Stop if the source, recipient or network is uncertain; a small test transfer does not replace verification.

1. Verify you are on the real website

  • Type the domain directly instead of following shortened links.
  • Check the Donate page and at least one additional official page for the same address.
  • Be skeptical of urgent private messages. The FTC warns that scammers often impersonate trusted organizations and push unusual payment methods, including cryptocurrency.

2. How do I verify a crypto charity donation address?

Open the live official Donate page, confirm the asset and network, and compare every character of the recipient address in your wallet with that page, including the middle. Matching only the first and last characters is not enough. Never copy a recipient from transaction history. Stop if the source, address or network is uncertain.

  • Confirm the chain or network before sending. BNB Chain, Ethereum, Bitcoin, and Solana addresses are not interchangeable.
  • For tokens, confirm the token contract on a recognized block explorer. ALI Token's BNB Smart Chain contract is visible on BscScan.

See the full-address safety check. An exact match with this site's instructions does not establish wallet ownership or charitable use.

3. A test transfer is not a substitute for verification

  • Only consider a small test after verifying the source, full recipient address, asset and network. If any check is unresolved, do not send.
  • Use stablecoins such as USDT when you want lower price volatility for aid budgeting, but still verify the chain and recipient address.

A successful test shows a transfer to that address, not who controls it or how funds will be used.

4. What proof should I keep after donating crypto?

Keep the full transaction hash and explorer URL, sender and recipient addresses, asset or token, exact amount, network, timestamp and status. A hash alone does not prove receipt; it is a lookup reference, not a donation receipt. An on-chain transfer does not establish charitable use, impact or tax eligibility.

  • Use the network-specific explorer linked from the Donate page. Check the actual asset or token transfer, recipient, amount and success/confirmation status on that network. Pending or failed transfers are not confirmed donations.
  • Save a dated copy of the donation page for your private records. ALI Charity's static site does not automatically record transfers, verify receipt or issue a tax receipt.
  • Review the transparency method separately. An unverified record is not verified receipt. Unknown totals remain unknown, not zero; different assets and networks must not be added into one unsupported total.

5. Watch for common scam patterns

  • "We changed our donation address, send here instead" without a verifiable official update.
  • Copycat domains, fake support accounts, and social posts using stolen logos.
  • Promises of returns, refunds, doubled donations, or guaranteed token price outcomes.
  • Recovery scams that claim your lost crypto has been traced and ask for fees to release it.
