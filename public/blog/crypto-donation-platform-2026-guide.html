How to choose a crypto donation platform in 2026

"Best platform" lists rarely tell you what matters. These seven criteria let you compare any crypto giving option — including ours — on evidence rather than marketing.

Published April 8, 2026Updated November 8, 2026By ALI Charity Operations7 min read

Short answer

Choose a platform that names the recipient organization, publishes one official address per asset and network on its own domain, never asks to connect or control your wallet, states fees and receipt policy in writing, and publishes dated reports that keep transfer records separate from spending and impact claims.

The three platform models

1. Direct charity address

The organization publishes its own wallet addresses. Simple and low-cost; receipts and reporting depend entirely on the organization.

2. Donation processor

A service accepts crypto on behalf of many nonprofits, often converting to fiat and issuing receipts. Check its fees and which entity issues the receipt.

3. Donor-advised fund (DAF)

You give to a fund that is itself a charity, then recommend grants. Useful for tax timing; grants are at the fund's discretion.

A five-minute due-diligence routine

  1. Type the platform's domain yourself and find its "about" and "contact" pages.
  2. Search the recipient's registration (for U.S. charities, the IRS Tax Exempt Organization Search).
  3. Read the fee and receipt policy before choosing an asset.
  4. Check that addresses appear only on the official domain and that the page never asks to connect your wallet.
  5. Look for dated reports and whether they distinguish transfers from spending and outcomes.

Frequently asked questions

What is the safest way to choose a crypto donation platform?

Prefer platforms that identify the recipient organization, publish one official address per asset and network, never ask to connect or control your wallet, explain fees and receipts in writing, and publish dated reports that separate transfers from spending and impact.

Do crypto donation platforms issue tax receipts?

Some do and some do not. Processors and donor-advised funds that are registered charities can usually issue receipts; a direct address published by an unregistered project cannot. Confirm before donating if a deduction matters to you.

Is ALI Charity a registered charity?

ALI Charity's registration and tax-deductible status are unverified; no registration evidence is published and the site does not issue tax receipts. It publishes official addresses, safety guidance and public records without connecting to donor wallets.

Does on-chain transparency prove a charity's impact?

No. On-chain records show that funds moved to an address. Spending, delivery and beneficiary outcomes need separate off-chain evidence such as receipts, partner reports or audits.
