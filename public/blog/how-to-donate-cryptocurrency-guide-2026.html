How to donate cryptocurrency to charity in 2026

Crypto donations are fast and borderless — and irreversible. The safe method is the same for every charity and every coin: verify first, send second, keep proof third.

Published April 8, 2026Updated November 8, 2026By ALI Charity Operations8 min read

Short answer

Open the charity's donation page by typing its official domain, choose a supported asset, confirm the exact network, copy the address from that live page and compare every character in your wallet, send from a wallet you control, then save the transaction hash and check it on the network's public explorer. If any check is unresolved, do not send.

Before you start: what you need

  • A wallet or exchange account you control, holding the asset you want to give.
  • Enough of the network's fee token (for example BNB for BNB Smart Chain transfers).
  • The recipient's official domain, typed by you — not a link from a message or ad.
  • If you want a tax deduction: confirmation that the recipient is a qualified organization where you pay tax.

The 7 steps

  1. Verify the recipient. Confirm the organization exists and that the donation page is on its own domain. Ignore "new address" messages on social media, chat or email.
  2. Check tax status if it matters to you. In the U.S., search the IRS Tax Exempt Organization Search and ask how receipts are issued. Do this before donating.
  3. Choose an asset and the exact network. The same token can exist on several networks. USDT on BNB Smart Chain is not the same destination as USDT on Ethereum or Tron.
  4. Copy and verify the full address. Copy from the live official page, paste into your wallet, and compare every character — including the middle. Matching only the first and last characters is not enough because address-poisoning scams create look-alike addresses.
  5. Send from your own wallet. A legitimate donation page never needs to connect to your wallet, request a signature, or ask for a seed phrase or private key.
  6. Save the transaction hash. Record the hash, date, asset, network and amount. This is your primary proof.
  7. Verify on a public explorer. Open the hash on the correct explorer (BscScan for BNB Smart Chain, mempool.space for Bitcoin, Solscan for Solana) and confirm the recipient matches the published address.

Tax records (U.S. example — not tax advice)

Whether a crypto gift is deductible depends on the recipient's status and your jurisdiction. In the U.S., IRS Publication 526 covers charitable contributions; donations of property such as cryptocurrency can carry extra documentation requirements, especially for larger amounts. Keep the transaction hash, the date and the fair-market value at the time of the gift, and ask the recipient for a written acknowledgment. Consult a tax professional for your situation.

Mistakes that cause permanent loss

  • Sending on a different network than the page lists.
  • Copying an address from transaction history, a screenshot or an old message.
  • Checking only the beginning and end of an address.
  • Connecting a wallet or signing a transaction on a "donation" site.
  • Responding to someone who offers to "recover" a mistaken transfer for a fee.

What a transaction hash proves — and what it doesn't

A hash proves that a transfer of a given amount reached a given address at a given time. It does not prove who legally controls that address, how funds were spent off-chain, or whether aid reached beneficiaries. Those claims need separate, dated reporting. Frequently asked questions

Can I get a tax deduction for donating crypto?

Only if the recipient is a qualified organization in your tax jurisdiction and you keep the records it requires. In the United States, IRS Publication 526 explains charitable deductions and the IRS Tax Exempt Organization Search can confirm status. ALI Charity's tax-deductible status is unverified.

What happens if I send crypto on the wrong network?

Funds sent on the wrong network or to an incorrect address are usually unrecoverable because blockchain transfers are irreversible. Always match the asset and network shown on the official donation page.

Should I send a small test transaction first?

Only after you have verified the source, the full recipient address, the asset and the network. A test transfer does not make an unverified address safe; if any check is unresolved, do not send.

Does a transaction hash prove my donation was used for charity?

No. A hash proves that a transfer was recorded to an address. Proof of spending and impact requires separate off-chain reporting from the organization.
