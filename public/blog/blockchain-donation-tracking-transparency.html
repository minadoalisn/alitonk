Transparency explainer

Blockchain donation tracking: what on-chain records prove — and what they don't

Public blockchains let anyone check that a donation arrived. They don't, on their own, show how it was spent. Knowing the difference is what makes "transparent giving" meaningful.

Published April 8, 2026Updated November 8, 2026By ALI Charity Operations6 min read

Short answer

Paste your transaction hash into the correct public explorer (BscScan, mempool.space or Solscan) to confirm the transfer succeeded and reached the full address published on the charity's official page. That proves the transfer — not who controls the address, how funds were spent off-chain, or what impact they had.

The three layers of donation evidence

Layer 1

Transfer

On-chain and public: sender, recipient, asset, amount, time, status. Verifiable by anyone with the hash.

Layer 2

Custody & spending

Who controls the address and where funds went next. Partly visible on-chain; conversions and purchases happen off-chain.

Layer 3

Impact

Delivery and beneficiary outcomes. Entirely off-chain: needs invoices, partner reports, photos with dates, or audits.

A platform that shows Layer 1 and implies Layers 2 and 3 is overstating its transparency. ALI Charity's transparency method publishes summaries only for reviewed periods and states that unknown does not mean zero.

How to track your donation in five steps

  1. Find the transaction hash (also called TXID) in your wallet or exchange withdrawal history.
  2. Open the correct explorer: BscScan for BNB Smart Chain, mempool.space for Bitcoin, Solscan for Solana.
  3. Check status and recipient. The transaction should be successful/confirmed, and the recipient must match the full address on the charity's live page.
  4. Check asset and amount. For tokens such as USDT, confirm the token contract shown is the genuine one for that network.
  5. Keep a record of the hash and explorer link with your notes.

Common misunderstandings

  • "Immutable means honest." A record that can't be changed can still record a transfer to the wrong or a fraudulent address.
  • "Tracked from wallet to beneficiary." Once funds are converted or spent off-chain, the blockchain stops telling the story.
  • "Live totals prove impact." Counters without source records and reporting periods prove nothing. Look for dated, reviewable data.
  • "Look-alike address is close enough." Scammers create addresses that match the first and last characters; compare every character (Etherscan on address poisoning).

Frequently asked questions

Can I see where my crypto donation went?

Yes, up to the receiving address. A public explorer shows the sender, recipient, amount, asset and time of a transfer. What happens after funds leave that address, especially off-chain, requires reporting from the organization.

Is blockchain donation tracking proof of impact?

No. On-chain tracking proves that a transfer happened. Proof of spending and beneficiary outcomes requires off-chain evidence such as invoices, partner confirmations, delivery records or audits.

Can anyone see my donation?

Yes. Transfers are public and pseudonymous: anyone can see the addresses and amounts, and an address can be linked to you if you reuse it or disclose it.
